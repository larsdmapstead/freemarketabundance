How it happens

Picture it.

A country where capitalism is finally allowed to work: free people, free markets, and a state that cannot stand in the way. You own your home, built in months by robots and builders who no longer wait on a permit. Your power bill is small enough to ignore. Your doctor, backed by AI, catches problems early and posts the price on the door. Your kid learns with a tutor that never runs out of patience and a world of friends to grow with. You work fewer hours and keep what you earn.

Nobody handed it out. Millions of free people built it, because the rules finally let them. That is free-market capitalism: the freedom to build, invent, trade and compete. AI, robots and cheap energy are the tools.

Who is in the way

Not you. The connected. Scarcity is manufactured, and we can name the factory: the permit office that sits on a new building for years, the license board that charges you to do work you already know how to do, the incumbent who hires a lobbyist to keep a competitor out, the rule that protects the big company from the small one. Behind every one of them is someone connected enough to get a rule written in their favor, and they gain every day you cannot build. We are on your side of that line, and we know their address.

How it happens

Technology replaces labor, increases output and collapses cost. Every time.

The tractor

A century ago most Americans worked the land and hunger was ordinary. The tractor replaced the farmhand, output soared, and food became so cheap that starvation in America is almost unheard of. Nobody voted for that. Somebody built it.

The Wright brothers

A month before they flew, experts said it would be fifty years before anyone did. Sixty-six years after Kitty Hawk, a man stood on the moon. The curve always looks impossible from the front.

The $5-an-hour worker

A humanoid robot that costs about $30,000 and works 100,000 hours is about $5 an hour of labor, energy and repairs included. Not just dishes and lawns: framing, wiring, plumbing, nursing. Labor is inside almost everything you buy. When labor collapses, prices follow.

The human genome

In 2001 reading one person’s full genome cost about $95 million. By 2022 it cost about $525. Nobody passed a law to make that happen. Every machine built made the next read cheaper, and every read taught the next machine. That is not an improvement. That is a collapse, and medicine is on the same curve now. NHGRI via Our World in Data

Labor and energy are the two biggest inputs in everything you buy. AI and robots collapse both. So every price falls, and your life gets better without a raise. The check doesn’t get bigger. The world gets cheaper.

Why capitalismSee the program

Say the word. Capitalism.

We are capitalists. We are proud of it, and by the end of this page you will see why you should be too.

Every plan for abundance ends in the same place: everyone lifted. Ours is the only one that gets there with nobody conscripted. Capitalism is voluntary all the way down. That is the whole difference, and it is the one that matters.

Capitalism is free people producing and trading by choice: owning what you build, choosing your work, investing your savings, and buying and selling by mutual agreement. Nobody is forced in. Nobody is locked out. Success is earned from customers, never bought from government.

Capitalism is you.

A nurse who opens her own clinic. A cashier who becomes a crew chief. A baker who hires six people. A worker who takes a better offer across town. Everyone who makes something, offers it to willing people and keeps the reward is part of it. And the boss of all of them is you: every dollar you spend or refuse to spend decides who runs the plants and who goes broke. The word belongs to builders and customers, and we are taking it back. Mises, Bureaucracy, 1944, pp. 20 to 21

Capitalism is not a bailout.

Subsidies, bailouts, special tax breaks and government stakes in private companies are not capitalism. They are favors for the connected, paid for by you, and the connected are the only people in this country who never have to earn a customer. We are against every one of them. The test is old and simple: does the law take from some people what belongs to them and hand it to others? Then it is plunder with a permit. A capitalist earns a customer. A crony buys a politician. Bastiat, The Law, 1850

Who pays?

Nobody has to. It is already being paid for, at five times the price.

Health care, covered, without a new program

America spends $5.3 trillion a year on health care. Government pays about $2.5 trillion of it; insurers, employers and families pay the rest. AI that reads the scan, designs the drug and catches the disease early cuts the cost of care by four fifths. At that price, real insurance for the catastrophe costs a fraction of today’s premium and everyone can carry it. The programs that exist today shrink as prices fall, nobody loses coverage before it gets cheaper, and the trillions government stops spending pay down the debt. Cheaper care. Everyone covered. Lower taxes. Nobody taxed more to do it. CMS, 2024

Courts, police and defense

Abolish the income tax and the agency that collects it. A government that only runs courts, police and defense does not need to know what you earn. It needs a small slice of what government spends today, raised by a low, visible levy that shrinks every year as cheaper everything shrinks what government has to do. Where a service can be paid for by the people who choose it, it is. The destination: you keep what you earn, all of it.
